Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It (Response)

As I read this astonishing article, the first thing that stuck out to me wasn’t necessarily the content, but the design. The consistent shift of colors across the black background (especially the flashes of red) made me feel an intense sense of dread and urgency, while simultaneously perpetuating an almost apocalyptic vibe.

The immersive, graphic design of this article is unreal, and to be completely honest, its visual elements were the first thing to stick out to me. The shifting colors, especially the flashes of red at the start, helped create an intense sense of dread and urgency, while simultaneously establishing an apocalyptic essence. In contrast to the black background, these shifts moved past subtlety, and instead exclaimed: “You need to pay attention.” Similarly, the detailed animations (like the shrinking missile, sudden explosion, burning flames, and black rain) go beyond the simple act of depicting utter chaos, and allow readers to actually place themselves in this “imaginary” scenario. In this sense, we begin to visualize what a nuclear war would be like, in real time.

Moving into the actual literary content, I was struck by the quote: “And while it may cause sleepless nights in Washington and Kyiv, most of the world has barely registered the threat.” This was a claim that I could personally relate to. I’ve always thought of nuclear weapons as a last-resort, “red-button” kind of situation. For our modern age, it just seems like a very extreme, unlikely, and unfavorable resource to use. So I was extremely surprised when the article began discussing the “playbook”, because this meant that these nuclear scenarios were no longer something hypothetical, but rather a real and calculated reality that the government was actively planning against. At the same time, I felt very frustrated. We live in a time where advanced military weapons and “cyberwarfare” dominate the battlefield, so why is there this sudden need to start a nuclear war? Why even have this as a serious option? It feels almost absurd that nations could consider falling back on something so destructive and dangerous, especially when possessing these weapons is meant to prevent a nuclear war, not start one. And what would they even be risking all of this for? More power? Control? I don't know, this logic just feels flawed.

Either way, by the end of this reading, I felt like this was an issue that doesn’t necessarily involve my opinion. Realistically, what can an average person do in this case? These are the kinds of decisions that lay in the hands of the Executive Branch and the Department of Defense, the higher powers. We, as individuals, don’t have enough sway to dissuade the government from viewing nuclear weapons as a viable option. So, despite this being a major threat, we don’t have a clear role in preventing anything.

We’re all uninformed on something that’s beyond our reach, that’s why it’s so easy to stay detached from it.
